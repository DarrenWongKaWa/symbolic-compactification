# Figure 2

**Title.** Claim semantics versus certificate provenance.

**Caption.**

Claim semantics \(\tau\) (what mathematical move is asserted) and
certificate provenance \(c\) (what evidence supports it) are independent
axes, not a ranking of truth. Four public combinations are shown:
algebra with engine `ZERO` yields `EXACT_ZERO`; substitution
\(\varepsilon_{21}=-\varepsilon_{12}\) with a child `ZERO` yields
`ZERO_UNDER_SUBSTITUTION`; Brillouin-zone integration by parts with a
local Leibniz `ZERO` and a declared torus rule yields
`CERTIFIED_BY_RULE`; an \(O(\Gamma)\) remainder without a remainder
certificate remains `UNKNOWN_REMAINDER`. Engine `ZERO` is not a rule
certificate. A finite coefficient `ZERO` is not a remainder proof.

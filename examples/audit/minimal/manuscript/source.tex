This note records coefficient identities for a toy Laurent polynomial
and an uncertified remainder claim. It is a synthetic public demo, not
a research manuscript.

Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F}
F(g) = \frac{a}{g} + b g,
\end{equation}
with $g\neq 0$. The coefficient of $g^{-1}$ is $a$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coeff-inv}
[g^{-1}]\,F(g) = a,
\end{equation}
and the coefficient of $g^{0}$ is $0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coeff-const}
[g^{0}]\,F(g) = 0.
\end{equation}
Those two coefficient identities are exact for this finite polynomial.

The global statement
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asymptotic}
F(g) = \frac{a}{g} + O(g)
\qquad (g\to 0)
\end{equation}
is an asymptotic remainder claim. Matching inspected coefficients does
not certify that the remainder vanishes, and the claim is not rewritten
as $F(g)-a/g=0$.
